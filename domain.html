Research behind the Smart Coffee Manufacturing System

This section presents the literature background, research gap, research problem, objectives, methodology and technologies used across the four research components.

01

Literature Survey

Existing research across coffee quality inspection and forecasting

The literature review covers computer vision, sensor-based monitoring, package-integrity inspection, sales forecasting, weather information and explainable machine learning. These areas provide the scientific and technical foundation for the four research components.

Component 01

Raw Coffee Bean Quality Assessment

Previous work demonstrates the value of visual characteristics such as colour, shape and visible defects for raw-bean assessment, while sensor measurements can provide additional sample-level information that is not available from images alone.

The proposed component therefore builds on computer vision and sensor-based assessment by treating visual and non-visual evidence as complementary inputs to a traceable pre-roast quality decision.

Bean defects Computer vision Sensor evidence Quality scoring
Component 02

Coffee Powder Quality Monitoring

Research on coffee powder quality includes moisture measurement, colour evaluation and environmental monitoring. These approaches show that moisture, temperature, humidity and colour can provide useful quality-related information.

The proposed component focuses on integrating selected low-cost measurements into a pre-packaging monitoring workflow with understandable batch decisions, rather than replacing laboratory reference methods.

Moisture RGB colour Temperature Humidity
Component 03

Packaging Quality & Leak Detection

Packaging research includes heat-seal integrity studies, machine-vision defect inspection and physical leak-testing methods. Visual methods can identify observable sealing abnormalities, while physical tests provide separate information about package integrity.

This distinction motivates a complementary approach in which visible seal defects and mechanical package responses are evaluated through different inspection paths before an overall quality decision is made.

Seal integrity YOLO detection Load response Physical testing
Component 04

Weather-Based Sales & Quality Forecasting

Forecasting literature shows the importance of historical sales, seasonal patterns and weather-related variables when studying future demand and production conditions. Machine learning can model nonlinear relationships among these factors.

Explainable AI is also important because operational users need to understand which factors influence a forecast, rather than receiving only a numerical prediction.

Sales forecasting Weather data Random Forest SHAP
02

Research Gap

Bridging isolated quality checks into an integrated manufacturing workflow

Existing studies and tools often address specific quality parameters or individual stages of coffee manufacturing. The central opportunity addressed by this research is to connect complementary forms of evidence and decision support across raw beans, powder, packaging and production forecasting in one coordinated smart manufacturing environment.

01

Raw Bean Gap

Visual inspection and sensor measurements are often treated separately. The project investigates how bean-level physical evidence, sensor observations, severity-aware scoring and explainable recommendations can be combined for batch-level pre-roast assessment.

02

Powder Monitoring Gap

Existing approaches commonly focus on individual measurements. The proposed work integrates moisture-related readings, temperature, humidity and supporting RGB information with PASS, WARN and HOLD decisions, corrective guidance and batch history.

03

Packaging Gap

Visual defect detection cannot by itself confirm physical package integrity. The research evaluates a practical combination of region-focused YOLO seal inspection and Arduino-based mechanical load-response testing.

04

Forecasting Gap

Sales forecasting, weather factors, quality prediction and explainability are frequently handled separately. The proposed component integrates historical sales, recent sales, weather data, machine learning, SHAP and decision support in one forecasting application.

03

Research Problem

Coffee quality decisions are fragmented across the manufacturing process.

Coffee manufacturing involves several quality-sensitive stages. When inspection relies heavily on manual judgement, isolated sensor readings, image-only inspection or separate forecasting methods, it becomes difficult to maintain consistent quality decisions, traceability and timely production planning.

The research therefore investigates how AI, sensor-based monitoring, physical testing, rule-based decision support and machine learning forecasting can be brought together to support a more automated and data-driven workflow from raw bean inspection to packaging and future planning.

Raw Beans

How can visual and sensor evidence be combined for consistent pre-roast batch assessment?

Powder

How can sensor readings be converted into clear packing-readiness decisions and corrective guidance?

Packaging

How can visual seal inspection and mechanical load-response testing provide complementary evidence?

Forecasting

How can historical sales and weather information support interpretable sales and quality predictions?

04

Research Objectives

A shared objective supported by four specialized research components

Main Objective

To develop an integrated smart coffee manufacturing system that combines artificial intelligence, sensor technologies, decision support and predictive analytics to support quality control and production planning from raw beans through packaging.

01

Raw Bean Assessment

Develop a pre-roast quality assessment workflow that combines sensor measurements, sample weight and multi-stage computer vision to identify physical bean conditions, generate quality scores and provide explainable batch-level decisions.

02

Powder Monitoring

Develop an Arduino-based monitoring system that collects moisture-related, RGB, temperature and humidity measurements; classifies production conditions as PASS, WARN or HOLD; and provides corrective and preventive guidance.

03

Packaging Inspection

Develop a two-stage YOLO visual inspection pipeline and a mechanical pressure-testing prototype, then integrate their outputs to support an overall coffee packaging quality decision.

04

Forecasting & Planning

Develop Random Forest models for sales and quality prediction using historical sales, previous-month sales, weather and seasonal features, while supporting twelve-month forecasting and SHAP-based explanation.

Physical Path

Visual Result + Physical Response → Combined Packaging Quality Assessment
